### Interview

**Settle the decisions that would change the diff, then hand the design to the execution playbook.** Run it before that playbook's step 1, from the main session, since a subagent's `AskUserQuestion` never reaches the user. Say that you are in Interview.

**Size the round by the playbook's row.** Pull it with `grep '^| <Playbook> |' <n-mode dir>/references/interview-axes.md`. Its cells are *Frontier must cover*, what the round settles, *Dispatch, never ask*, what you look up, and *Design must name*. If two playbooks fit, fold in the second row and say why. **Opening a PR** inherits its playbook's design, and **figure-it-out** borrows the closest row. Ask only what the request, the repo, and any spec leave open and what would change the diff, citing the spec section a gap comes from. A request that states the change, its site, and its cause (a diagnosed bug, a mechanical rename, one flag on one command) often leaves nothing open. The *Operations* rows (Babysit, Shipping, Pause safely, Worktree cleanup) take one question or none. "Interview me", "grill me", or "phỏng vấn" asks the whole frontier.

**An opt-out** ("just pick and go") **or a full-autonomy grant** ("going to bed", "run until done", "be fully autonomous") asks nothing. After step 1, list every decision as yours and go to step 5. Never opt out for the user.

**Never a question.** Facts are yours: anything you can learn by reading or running gets a trace, a subagent, or a sketch per **Prototype**. Process is yours: the worktree, branch, landing, verification, review passes, and tooling, so list them. Neither never-block nor the prototype trigger replaces this round.

1. Trace the affected subsystem yourself before the first round. Hand bulk reading to one `nstack:n-agent` told not to edit, on the Explore and search row per **Subagents** in `SKILL.md`, and keep its summary.
2. The frontier is every open decision whose prerequisites are settled. Ask all of it in one round through `AskUserQuestion`: your recommended answer first, `(Recommended)` in its label, the tradeoff in its description, and no "Other" option, since the tool adds one. Past the per-call limit, make sequential calls with nothing between. Use numbered markdown only for open-ended questions, such as "paste the error", with your recommendation when one exists. After step 1, questions that wait on a running subagent go to the next round.
3. Recompute the frontier and ask again until every branch of the row is visited and every decision is answered or left open by the user.
4. **Wait for the user to confirm the design** when any answer differs from your recommendation, free text in place of an option included, or a question is still open. An answer to a question with no recommendation, such as a pasted error, is an input, not a deviation. Otherwise state the design in the reply and proceed.
5. Re-match the execution playbook against the design, then open the todo list with its steps copied in verbatim. A step that repeats step 1's trace reuses it.
6. Name the branch, unless a coordinator row opens none, write the intent, open the worktree per **Worktree** in `SKILL.md`, and start the playbook's step 1. The intent is `<root>/.nstack/intents/<slug>.md`, named and kept per the **nstack folder** section of `../references/worktree.md`. Its sections, and nothing else: **Problem** in the user's words, **Outcome** checkable by someone else, **Surfaces**, **Constraints**, **Out of scope**, **Decisions** as two lists (the user's and yours), and **Open questions** with what would settle each. It answers the row's *Design must name* cell.

**Who writes the intent.** Every *Build and change* row except **Prototype**, and the coordinator rows (Multi-phase plan, Autopilot-full, Autopilot-stack). Prototype, the read-only rows, and the *Operations* rows keep the design in the reply. **Session pickup** amends the file it inherits. When execution changes course, amend the file and say what moved.

**Reply:** the design, the user's decisions and yours, what is out of scope, the intent path, and the execution playbook.
